# Major Airline Synthetic Case — Public Calibration Basis

## Purpose

The **Northstar Global Airlines (Synthetic)** case is a fictional major-airline underwriting proof of value. It is designed to demonstrate how StrategicRisk Partners could reconcile a large carrier's submission, fleet schedule, insurance evidence, registry evidence, event history, filing evidence, and fleet-plan information before an authorized underwriting decision.

It does **not** represent United Airlines' actual insurance program, fleet values, limits, deductibles, losses, claims, reinsurance structure, premium, underwriting information, or confidential data.

## Public source used for scale calibration

Primary source:

- United Airlines Holdings, Inc. / United Airlines, Inc.
- Quarterly Report on Form 10-Q
- Quarter ended March 31, 2026
- Public filing, especially MD&A pages 20–24

Publicly reported Q1 2026 operating scale included:

- 42.486 million passengers;
- 63.385 billion revenue passenger miles;
- 77.698 billion available seat miles;
- 81.6% passenger load factor;
- 1.093 billion gallons of fuel consumed;
- 115,600 employees;
- USD 14.608 billion of operating revenue;
- USD 854 million of aircraft maintenance materials and outside-repair expense; and
- 16.8% year-over-year growth in aircraft maintenance materials and outside-repair expense.

The filing also reported substantial aircraft purchase commitments. As of March 31, 2026, the public table showed firm commitments for 787, 737 MAX 9, 737 MAX 10, A321neo, A321XLR, and A350 aircraft. The public table totaled 600 firm commitments, with expected deliveries of 87 in the last nine months of 2026, 89 in 2027, and 379 after 2027.

The filing describes additional risk context including geopolitical disruption and fuel-price pressure, possible regulatory constraints on capacity, government funding/staffing disruptions, supplier and delivery dependence, technology-system disruption, cybersecurity/privacy obligations, and the possibility of higher insurance costs or inadequate coverage.

## How the synthetic case differs

Northstar is deliberately fictional.

The prototype uses:

- 1,050 synthetic aircraft;
- synthetic N-number-like identifiers;
- synthetic serial numbers;
- synthetic hull values;
- a synthetic USD 2.25 billion requested liability limit;
- fictional losses and aviation events;
- fictional delegated-authority thresholds;
- fictional insurance-certificate evidence;
- fictional fleet-delivery assumptions; and
- fictional hub/network labels.

The fleet mix is designed to resemble the diversity of a large U.S. network carrier rather than reproduce any public airline's exact in-service fleet.

## Why use public financial and operating information?

A large-airline underwriting workflow must reason across more than individual aircraft. Public operating disclosures can help demonstrate the kinds of scale signals an aviation underwriter may want to reconcile with a submission, such as:

- passenger and capacity scale;
- global network footprint;
- fleet transition and delivery volume;
- maintenance-cost pressure;
- fuel exposure;
- supplier concentration;
- hub and infrastructure dependence;
- financing and asset encumbrance;
- geopolitical exposure; and
- technology / cyber dependence.

Public disclosures provide scenario calibration only. They are not substitutes for actual underwriting evidence supplied by an insured, broker, carrier, or reinsurer.

## Prototype use

The Northstar case is intended to test:

1. whether 1,050 aircraft can be normalized and reconciled deterministically;
2. whether evidence mismatches can be traced to source records;
3. whether fleet transition discrepancies can be identified;
4. whether external aviation events are handled conservatively;
5. whether large arrays can be compacted before AI synthesis without changing deterministic results;
6. whether the system preserves human disposition and auditability; and
7. whether the same architecture can scale from commercial GA to a major-airline workflow.
