# AeroCIE | Severity evidence and buyer conversations

## Commercial narrative

**Headline:** Which aviation submissions contain severity indicators that deserve another look before you quote?

**Purpose:** Make potential cost pressure, missing source evidence, and assumptions visible. Support an underwriter's judgement and triage process. The product does not claim to measure premium adequacy or prevent a loss.

**Three buyer screens:**
1. Repair stress sensitivity: low, base and high synthetic assumptions, with an explicit formula, parts index, wait days, and hull value.
2. Review priority: Quote, Refer and Decline are illustrative triage labels with factor contributions and missing-data gates. All decisions remain with the carrier's underwriting authority.
3. Evidence lineage: data source, observation date, confidence limits and source freshness. Missing or stale evidence leaves the scenario unavailable.

## Pilot proposal

Offer a $2,500, 60-day evaluation on fictional or properly authorized historic submissions. Agree on submission volume, acceptable source licenses, evaluation endpoints, baseline workflow time, adverse outcome definition and confidentiality before accepting real files. Keep result reviewers blind to actual outcomes until scoring has been saved. Test calibration, missing values and subgroup differences rather than claiming a score predicts claims.

Collect practical buying signals: willingness to allocate 50 cases; time for source review; a sponsor who owns GA book performance; written permission for an evaluation; and a budget range. A 20-30% close rate across unsolicited outreach is not established for AeroCIE. Track connect-to-meeting, meeting-to-pilot and pilot-to-contract conversion separately.

## Interview prompts for aviation underwriting managers

- Show the last submission where repair costs, part availability or turnaround complicated your assessment.
- Which data do you trust enough to use in an approval or referral decision, and who verifies it?
- When a facility cannot supply a current wait-time observation, how do underwriters respond?
- What is the financial consequence of follow-up review, and what measured delay is acceptable?
- Which underwriting authority should define a referral threshold?
- If this display saves time or catches undocumented exposure, what evidence would justify a paid pilot?
