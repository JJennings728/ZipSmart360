# Aviation Portfolio Visual Style

## Page 1 — Cover

### Hero image
Use the licensed exterior commercial-aircraft image as the full-width hero image across approximately 40–45% of the page.

Recommended treatment:

- preserve the aircraft as the dominant focal point;
- retain open sky for copy space;
- apply a subtle dark navy-to-transparent overlay from left to right;
- avoid covering aircraft details with text.

### Title placement
Upper-left within the hero image.

**Aviation Underwriting Portfolio**

Recommended hierarchy:

- large white title;
- smaller white subtitle directly underneath;
- optional small kicker above: `SPECIALTY RISK ANALYTICS`.

### Subtitle
**Exposure Analysis · Pricing · Capacity · Risk Decision Support**

### Metric strip
Place immediately below the hero image as a four-column band.

| Metric | Supporting label |
|---|---|
| Fleet Exposure | Asset & portfolio view |
| Hull & Liability | Coverage structure |
| Premium & Rate Analysis | Pricing framework |
| Capacity & Controls | Underwriting discipline |

### Page-one content
Begin the portfolio overview immediately below the metric strip.

Suggested opening:

> A synthetic aviation underwriting case study focused on exposure analysis, pricing structure, capacity, underwriting assumptions, subjectivities, and decision support for complex specialty risks.

## Interior page — cockpit image

### Page title
**Operational Risk & Underwriting Decision Support**

### Image placement
Use the licensed cockpit-at-night image as a wide horizontal banner across approximately the upper third of the page.

The image should remain visually dominant without obscuring the instrument panel.

### Content beneath image
Use a two-column analytical layout covering:

**Operational risk**
- fleet utilization;
- geographic operations;
- maintenance / MRO information;
- safety-management information;
- pilot / crew and operating controls;
- loss history and incident context.

**Underwriting decision support**
- submission gaps;
- subjectivities;
- capacity constraints;
- pricing assumptions;
- human-review triggers;
- documentation required before binding.

### Narrative role
The cover image represents **what is insured**.  
The cockpit image represents **how complex operational risk is evaluated**.

## Stock-image handling

Licensed stock source files should not be committed to the public repository as standalone downloadable assets.

The GitHub repository should document:

- asset purpose;
- placement;
- visual treatment;
- licensed-source identifier where useful; and
- the final derivative portfolio artifact when appropriate under the applicable license.

Keep original licensed source files in private working storage.
