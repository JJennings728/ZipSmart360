# Reinsurance and War Risk

## Purpose

Demonstrate awareness that direct aviation underwriting operates within a broader insurance and reinsurance environment.

## Reinsurance context

The historical Marsh Q4 2024 report described:

- adequate capacity for much aviation business;
- increased appetite in some reinsurance segments;
- continued sensitivity to loss costs;
- rising hull repair costs and supply disruption;
- liability pressure from large court awards;
- continuing Russia-Ukraine leasing uncertainty; and
- downward risk-adjusted pricing pressure in some reinsurance programs.

## Hull War

The portfolio should track:

- hull values;
- areas of operation;
- fleet aggregation;
- risk mitigation;
- war-sensitive territories;
- modeled war premium;
- capacity assumption; and
- referral trigger.

## Excess AVN52

The portfolio should document:

- underlying liability limit;
- excess attachment;
- requested excess limit;
- reinstatement assumption if relevant;
- capacity assumption;
- modeled premium;
- geography / war-risk considerations; and
- referral conditions.

## What this section does not do

It does not reproduce proprietary policy wording or market quotations.

It does not claim live reinsurance pricing or current market capacity.

It demonstrates how an underwriter should recognize when direct-account decisions depend on war-risk and reinsurance considerations.
